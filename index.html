VR&E Approval Prep Tool

Your rating, where you are in the process, what you want to study, and what it costs. Back comes your prep packet: what's documented and what's thin, who has to approve your plan, and a letter to your counselor drafted from your own answers.

Veteran Readiness and Employment (VR&E, Chapter 31) can pay full tuition with no cap, books, and a monthly allowance without spending your GI Bill. It is also the most counselor-dependent benefit VA runs: two veterans with the same rating and the same school can get opposite answers. The rules are written down. 38 CFR 21.72 trains a veteran to the level needed for entry into a suitable goal. 38 CFR 21.120(c) makes cost a factor only when a cheaper school meets the veteran's needs equally. VA's manual requires the counselor to answer four written questions before choosing the cheaper school, and above $50,000 a year a memo has to be approved above the counselor before the plan is signed. This tool checks your case against those rules, shows who has to approve your plan, drafts a letter to your counselor from your own answers, and pairs each common objection with the regulation that answers it. It cannot read your rating decision or judge your case. That takes a person.

How this tool works

the rules it checks · what the letter is · what this isn't

The readiness check. Eight elements, each marked documented, thin, or missing from your answers: basic eligibility (a service-connected rating and, for discharges before 2013, the 12-year window in 38 U.S.C. 3103), an employment handicap tied to specific limits on work, a defined job goal, a training level that matches what the occupation requires for entry (38 CFR 21.72), the months of entitlement the program needs against the 48-month ceiling and the months you have already used (38 CFR 21.78), a school and program that VA has approved (the counselor verifies both in WEAMS), a cost that either stays under the counselor's own authority or comes with the justification ready, and evidence in hand. There is no percentage and no approval probability, because no data exists to support one. The check tells you where your case is thin so you can fix it before the appointment.

Who approves your plan. VA's manual sets a counselor's approval authority at $50,000 a year in program costs (tuition, fees, books, and supplies over the 12 months after the plan is written; subsistence excluded). Above that a high program costs memo is required and must be approved before the plan is signed: by the VR&E Officer up to $75,000, the Regional Office Director up to $100,000, and the Executive Director of VR&E Service above $100,000 (M28C.V.B.1.02.b, tiers as revised June 18, 2026). Costs are rechecked at every annual review. Where a cheaper school exists, M28C.IV.C.2.03.a requires the counselor to consider your preference and to answer four written questions before writing the plan; two of the four are about support services and disability programs you will use, so the tool builds your answers on the limits you checked, not on salary or prestige.

The letter. The Vocational Goal and Rehabilitation Plan Statement follows the seven-section structure The Better Veteran uses with one-on-one clients: background, employment handicap, vocational goal, why this facility for your disability, why this degree, feasibility, and the request. The tool fills in what it knows from your answers and leaves a bracket wherever it needs your real information: the exact wording from your rating decision, your work history, the school's accommodations. Everything you type into the letter stays in your browser. It is never sent to us, never placed in the share link, and never in the emailed results link; the link rebuilds your selections and the letter with its brackets. Nothing here is legal advice, the counselor makes the determinations under 38 CFR 21.294, and this tool cannot read your rating decision or tell you whether your case is strong. That takes a person.
